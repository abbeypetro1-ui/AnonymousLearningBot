## What is conflict and can healthy conflict resolution be learned?
Conflict is the process of disagreement. Healthy conflict resolution can be learned. It is only possible if both parties can communicate. In other words, resolving conflict in a healthy way depends on both sides being able to communicate. This is part of Chapter 8, which starts with defining conflict and then moves on to the resolution phases.

## What are the 4 I's of conflict?
The 4 I's of conflict are four items listed in the notes: Interdependence, Incompatible goals, Insufficient resources, and Interference. Each of the four starts with the letter I. The notes list these four items as a numbered list: 1. Interdependence, 2. Incompatible goals, 3. Insufficient resources, 4. Interference. No further definitions of the four are given in the notes.

## What are the phases of conflict resolution?
Conflict resolution has two phases in the notes. The first phase is Differentiation, where people share perspectives to create understanding and identify points of difference. The second phase is Integration, where people recognize common ground and explore possible solutions. The movement goes from Differentiation to Integration. The phases are listed in this order: 1. Differentiation, 2. Integration.

## What is differentiation in conflict resolution?
Differentiation is the first phase of conflict resolution. It means sharing perspectives to create understanding and to identify points of difference. The goal is understanding perspective. The notes list these problems/barriers for this phase: defensiveness causing escalation, malevolent cycling, conflict spirals, and avoidance. The notes also ask: what roles are people playing? Differentiation comes before Integration.

## What is integration, and how do you shift from differentiation to integration?
Integration is the second phase of conflict resolution. It involves recognizing common ground and exploring possible solutions. The notes describe the move from Differentiation to Integration as a shift from input to process. The notes say to explicitly signal this shift. The notes also include the phrase "Could be done > should be done."

The notes use the heading Interests > Positions. Under it, the notes list three points: ask "why" and "why not", recognize multiple interests, and look for warning signs. ## What is communicating dialogically?
Communicating dialogically means using dialogue. Dialogue is collaborative communication where partners create shared meaning. The notes list four parts: establish foundations of respect, including mutual equality; a supportive climate, with attention to tone (warm or defensive); listening effectively by giving attention, limiting judgement, and clarifying; and expressing your points by stating opinions in ways that invite dialogue.

## What are Gibb's communication behaviors that create a defensive or supportive climate?
Gibb's 6 communication behaviors create either a defensive or a supportive climate. The notes list three pairs: Evaluation vs Description (evaluation is negative judgement, description is facts); Neutrality vs Empathy (neutrality is indifference, empathy is understanding); and Certainty vs Provisionalism (certainty is rigid or close-minded, provisionalism is open-minded). Each pair has two behaviors, which makes six in total.

## What is destructive conflict, and what is covert conflict?
In the notes, destructive conflict is listed with two types, covert and overt, and the notes also list the demand/withdraw sequence under it. Covert conflict means messages that deny that conflict exists. The notes list four forms of covert conflict: denial, displacement, disengagement, and fake mutuality. These four forms all appear under the Covert heading.

## What is overt destructive conflict?
Overt conflict is conflict that is made obvious in a toxic manner. The notes describe it as including insulting, violence, and threats. The notes list four related items under it: "sudden-death" statements, harmful secrets, chilling effect, and negatively spirals. Overt is the second type of destructive conflict in the notes, after covert.

## What do avoidance, accommodating, competing, collaborating, and compromising mean in conflict?
The notes list five terms, each defined by the level of concern for self and for others. Avoidance: low concern for self, low concern for others. Accommodating: low concern for self, high concern for others. Competing: high concern for self, low concern for others. Collaborating: high concern for self, high concern for others. Compromising: medium concern for self, medium concern for others.
